How to use this chart
  1. Follow this cycle: pick the past cycle you think the coin is repeating, or leave it on Auto to follow the closest match. Its line goes thick, and the stats, targets, roadmap and calendar switch to it.
  2. Read a price: hover over the chart, or tap and drag on a phone. The box under the chart shows the month and each line's price, plus the change from today.
  3. Cycle bottom: "Already in" assumes the current cycle low holds. "Retest" drops price to a new low in December first; type the low you expect.
  4. Peak timing: "Own clock" copies the old cycle's timing exactly. "Halving clock" lines each cycle's halving up with the April 2028 Bitcoin halving, which puts the top around Sep–Oct 2029. "Late Q4" pushes the top to Dec 2029.
  5. Sizing: "Scaled" shrinks every replay to this cycle's likely size (shown in the chart note). "Same % as original" repeats the old percentage gains, which gets unrealistic for older cycles.
  6. Chips: tap a coloured chip to hide or show that line. The % on each chip is how closely 2026 has tracked that cycle so far.

What confirms or breaks the bull case

    How the model works

    Replays. Each historical replay copies that cycle's weekly route from bear low to top, then its following bear. "Own clock" keeps the original spacing between low, halving and top. The halving clock stretches it so the halving lands on April 2028.

    Sizing. Each cycle's gain, measured in log terms, has shrunk at a steady rate. Scaled replays reach the midpoint of two decay models: new highs shrinking at the top-to-top rate, and runs off the bottom shrinking at their rate. "Same % as original" repeats each cycle's raw multiple instead.

    Institutional cycle. A hypothetical path, not a replay: if ETFs and treasuries smooth the cycle, the top comes about a year later than the halving clock, the climb is steadier, and the following bear is about −30% instead of −50% or worse.
